Law of Sines

Today we derive and apply the Law of Sines by connecting triangle area, height, and opposite angle-side pairs.

Learning Target

I understand how to derive and apply the Law of Sines.

Main Idea
sin(A)/a = sin(B)/b = sin(C)/c
IntroDerivationLawExampleLaser PracticeQuick Check
Intro

Start with height.

When a height is dropped inside a non-right triangle, it creates a right triangle. That lets us use sine.

sin(72°) = h/8.4

So the height is:

h = 8.4sin(72°)
Derivation

Three area expressions lead to the Law of Sines.

Using angle A

h = bsin(A)

Area = 1/2 · c · bsin(A)

Using angle B

h = csin(B)

Area = 1/2 · a · csin(B)

Using angle C

h = asin(C)

Area = 1/2 · b · asin(C)

Because all three represent the same area:

1/2bc sin(A) = 1/2ac sin(B) = 1/2ab sin(C)

Cancel the 1/2, divide everything by abc, and you get the Law of Sines.

Law of Sines

Opposite pairs matter.

sin(A)/a = sin(B)/b = sin(C)/c

Worked Example

Find the length of side AC.

Match opposite pairs.

Side 11.7 is opposite 41°. Side b is opposite 105°.

Set up the proportion.

sin(41°)/11.7 = sin(105°)/b

Cross multiply and solve.

b sin(41°) = 11.7sin(105°)

b = 11.7sin(105°)/sin(41°)

Answer.

b ≈ 17.25

Practice

Charlie and Quinn’s lasers

Charlie and Quinn are 100 ft apart. Quinn points his laser at an angle of elevation of 65°, and Charlie points his laser at an angle of elevation of 40°. Quinn’s laser is 60 ft long when they intersect.

Find the third angle.

180° − 65° − 40° = 75°

Find Charlie’s laser length.

x/sin(65°) = 60/sin(40°)

x = 60sin(65°)/sin(40°)

x ≈ 84.6 ft

Find the height.

h = 60sin(65°)

h ≈ 54.4 ft
